Issues ranked by severity:

1. **High — the reported speed omits preprocessing required by the protocol.**

   The fetch script [precomputes 10 fps video chunks](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/scripts/fetch_test_videos.py:17). Tracking then [reuses those cached chunks](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/etho/tracking/pipeline.py:70). Every test video’s recorded `prep_s` is approximately 0.0005–0.001 seconds, and the chunk files predate tracking. Yet [EVAL_PLAN §4.4](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/docs/EVAL_PLAN.md:277) expressly includes chunking.

   The reported arithmetic—7,192.085 seconds / 8,736.9 seconds = **0.823185×**—is correct for the recorded stages. It is incomplete for the registered metric. Filesystem timestamps suggest approximately **1,790 additional summed per-video seconds** of preprocessing, bringing the ratio to about **1.03×**. That is forensic evidence, not a replacement stopwatch measurement, but it strongly challenges “minimum speed: MET.”

   Voiding the scorer’s cache-replay timing was correct. The reconstructed tier-1 boundaries agree with the ledger and window counts. However, that reconstruction does not fix missing preprocessing or establish a strict conservative bound.

   **Fix:** withdraw the minimum-speed MET verdict pending a complete accounting. Preserve the original artifacts and add a correction. Safe interim wording:

   > “With video chunks precomputed, summed tracking, rules, and adjudication time was approximately 0.82 times video duration. Preprocessing time is excluded; the registered complete-processing speed target has not been established.”

   A separately disclosed preprocessing-only measurement would not require model calls or accuracy rescoring.

2. **High — the headline and “untouched” footnote are not safe as written.**

   “No labeled examples” can reasonably mean no labeled data influenced the system. That is false: labeled development data selected its settings. More seriously, [the plan explicitly acknowledges](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/docs/EVAL_PLAN.md:33) that test-label summaries influenced development-set selection, the sampling rate, and targets. Calling these “19 untouched videos,” while relegating that disclosure to longer material, gives readers the wrong impression.

   “Training-free” is defensible only as **no task-specific weight training or fine-tuning**, not absence of supervision. “Plain-English behavior definitions” is supported.

   **Replace the headline with:**

   > “Plain-English behavior definitions, no task-specific model training or fine-tuning, and no labeled examples supplied to models in the primary arm.”

   **Replace the footnote with:**

   > “Pipeline settings were selected using 13 labeled development videos. Predictions were evaluated once on all 19 official test videos. Before evaluation, test-label summaries informed development-set selection, sampling rate, and targets.”

   Also replace “no setting was tuned on test” with the narrower supported statement: **“No test prediction scores were used to select this configuration.”**

3. **Medium — the protocol was substantially followed, with qualifications that must remain visible.**

   Evidence supports one scored evaluation: the ledger contains one test-1 scoring execution and one subsequent identity execution. All four arms were validated and hashed before labels loaded. The inspected product-code diffs against the frozen commit are empty.

   The crash-and-resume is defensible. All 3,241 successful primary-arm request keys appear in cache replay; no request key had multiple successful uncached responses during the test inference window. The unavailable timed-out response was requested again on restart. This supports **“one scored evaluation, resumed after an inference failure,”** not one uninterrupted process or one API attempt.

   Nevertheless:

   - Configuration and numeric targets preceded SAM inference, but complete gate reconciliation did not: SAM began at 08:38; A1 followed at 08:49, with another scorer change at 08:54.
   - Tracking’s execution commit and dirty state were overwritten rather than preserved; they are inferred.
   - The failure is preserved in the traceback, decision log, and spend ledger, but not a structured failed-attempt record.

   These are provenance/reporting shortcomings, not evidence of favorable result selection. **The crash does not justify replacing test-1 with a new headline test-2.** Nor does a timing-accounting defect justify rerunning accuracy.

4. **Medium — “73%” is arithmetically valid, but the references need precise names and context.**

   I verified the published **0.793 ± 0.011** raw-pose 1D ConvNet baseline, its 70-video training set, and the matching pooled frame-level macro-F1 convention. The ± value is standard deviation across five training runs. This is a reasonable benchmark comparison, although the different inputs and development procedures prevent attributing the performance gap specifically to training.

   The report’s missing per-class references can be resolved: Table 8 gives attack **0.664**, investigation **0.814**, and mount **0.900**. The paper also reports stronger methods, so 0.793 must not stand for supervised performance generally or state of the art. [Sun et al., Tables 1, 2, 8 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.02710v4)

   Safe wording:

   > “Etho achieved 73.1% of the published raw-pose 1D ConvNet baseline’s macro F1: 0.580 versus 0.793, a 0.213 gap.”

   The transformed interval, **62.1%–78.3%**, treats 0.793 as fixed; it is not a complete uncertainty interval for the comparison. Keep the required per-behavior results and supervision disclosure beside the ratio.

   The **0.134491** reference is correctly calculated for a frequency-matched random predictor. It is not a universal chance floor: using the same disclosed prevalences, uniform guessing over four classes yields approximately **0.16198** macro F1; uniform guessing over the three scored behaviors yields **0.17904**.

   Label it:

   > “Frequency-matched random reference: approximately 0.1345 macro F1, calculated from test-set label prevalences.”

   Avoid “4.3× chance” or presenting this as the strongest no-information baseline.

6. **Low — fix smaller factual and statistical presentation problems.**

   - **Few-shot activity count:** [the report says 2,117 changed windows](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/eval/results/test1_report.md:190). The verdict files support **1,829**. The difference is 288 `other` → `none` verdicts, which [map to the same label](/home/ubuntu/work/etho/etho/adjudication/tier1.py:179).
   - **Nonexclusive ablation:** the runner resolves overlapping detectors before serialization. One-vs-rest metrics reconstructed from those exclusive predictions cannot establish the effect of removing priority. Say: **“Pre-priority behavior masks were not retained; a true nonexclusive ablation was not computed.”**
   - **Rounding:** truncating every bound downward is not uniformly conservative. In particular, changing a negative upper bound from −0.000067 to −0.0001 makes a barely excluded zero look farther away. Use conventional rounding or outward-rounded intervals, retaining extra precision near zero.
   - The few-shot mount decrease is a **nominal, borderline interval result**, among multiple comparisons. Do not portray it as a robust finding.
